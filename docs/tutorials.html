Get started

Interactive tutorials

Phoenix can teach itself. A lesson walks you through a task one step at a time: a small window says what to do, and the control to use next is circled on the screen.

Start a lesson

  1. Choose Help › Tutorials..., or click Tutorials... on the start screen.
  2. Pick a lesson. Each shows a picture of how it turns out, how long it takes, what it is about and whether you have finished it; the one picked plays its picture beside the list. The buttons along the top (Animation, Motion Graphics, Rigging and the rest) show only the lessons about that; All shows them all again.
  3. Click Start. The lesson opens a new document of its own, so your work is never touched.

Most steps wait for you to do what they ask, such as choosing a tool, drawing a shape or inserting a keyframe, and move on by themselves when you have. Next skips a step, and Previous goes back one, undoing what that step did. Drag the step window anywhere; it stays where you put it.

To see how a lesson is meant to turn out, click Show Final in the step window, or select the lesson in the Tutorials dialog and click Show Final there. The finished document opens as an untitled copy in a tab of its own, so you can compare it with yours and switch back to carry on.

Close a lesson with its ✕ button or Esc. Closing the lesson's document ends it too. The document you made stays open, so you can save it if you want to keep it.

Tip

If a step points at something that is not on the screen, such as a panel you have closed, the step window says how to get it back. Next always moves on.

Write your own lessons

A lesson is a folder holding a lesson.json file, and, if it starts from prepared artwork, that document. Put the folder in the tutorials folder in Phoenix's application data folder; the Tutorials dialog shows where that is and opens it. A lesson there with the same folder name as a built-in one takes its place, which lets a studio adapt a lesson to its own pipeline.

{
  "title": "Our cut-out rig",
  "summary": "Pose the studio's standard character.",
  "minutes": 8,
  "order": 50,
  "tags": ["Rigging", "Animation"],
  "start": {"document": "start"},
  "steps": [
    {
      "title": "Pick the Selection tool",
      "text": "<p>Press <kbd>V</kbd>.</p>",
      "target": "tool:Selection",
      "wait": {"tool": "Selection"}
    }
  ]
}
tags
What the lesson is about, to find it by in the Tutorials dialog: the same names as the built-in lessons (Getting Started, Drawing, Animation, Symbols, Rigging, Motion Graphics, Effects, Text, Templates, Scripting, Sound, 3D) or new ones of your own.
thumbnail
Not a setting but a file: a thumbnail.gif (which plays when the lesson is picked) or thumbnail.png in the lesson's folder, 240 by 135 pixels or so. tools/lesson_thumbnails.py in Phoenix's source makes one from a lesson's final.
start
{"new": {"width": 1280, "height": 720, "fps": 24}} for a new document, or {"document": "start"} for a document in the lesson's folder: an XFL folder, an .fla or a .phx. It opens as an untitled copy.
text
Rich text: paragraphs, <b>, <i>, links, and <kbd> for keys. A step can also have an image, a picture or animated GIF in the lesson's folder.
target
What to circle: tool:Oval; a menu command, menu:Insert/Timeline/Keyframe; a panel, dock:Library; a panel's tab, tab:Properties/Frame; a field by its label, label:Properties/Ease; timeline frames, frame:Layer_1:24 or frame::2-23 for the top layer; the ruler, ruler:12; the stage or a part of it in document pixels, stage:60,240,320,320; or a control by name, such as widget:timelinePlay.
final
Optional: the document as the lesson leaves it (or just before a last step, such as baking, that would hide how it was made), in the lesson's folder, the same kinds as start: "final": "final". With it, the reader can click Show Final.
wait
What moves the lesson on: {"tool": "Oval"}; {"command": "Convert to Symbol"}, an edit by the name Edit › Undo shows for it; {"action": "File/Export/Export Movie"}, a menu command run; {"frame": 24}; {"selection": "shape"} (or instance, text, any, none); {"scope": "symbol"} or "scene"; or {"playing": true}. Without one, the reader clicks Next.

To check a lesson without stepping through it, run Phoenix lessons --check (see Command line): it lists every lesson and any target or wait that Phoenix cannot find.
