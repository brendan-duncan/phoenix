Coming from Animate

If you know Adobe Animate or Flash, you already know most of Phoenix. This page covers the differences, so you can get straight to work.

What carries over

  • Your files. Phoenix opens FLA and XFL files, and older Flash files, and saves FLA files that Animate opens again. Data Phoenix does not use itself, such as ActionScript and publish settings, is kept as it was.
  • The drawing model. Merge and object drawing, primitives, groups, and how strokes and fills interact all behave as you expect.
  • The timeline. Layers, folders, keyframes, classic, shape and motion tweens, eases, masks, guides and motion guides.
  • Symbols. Graphics, movie clips and buttons, editing in place, looping options, color effects, blend modes and filters.
  • Your muscle memory. Tool keys, F5 to F8, the frame and arrange shortcuts, and the modifier keys while drawing are the same.

What works differently

  • One document at a time. Phoenix shows one document per window. Opening another replaces it, after asking about unsaved changes.
  • Native format. New documents save as .phx, which opens much faster. Use Save As to write an FLA whenever you need one.
  • Movie clips on the stage show their first frame while you edit; their animation plays in exports.
  • Scripts are kept, not run. ActionScript and frame scripts are saved with the file, but Phoenix does not run them.

What Phoenix adds

Ink Brush
A brush with a string stabilizer and adjustable pressure response, for clean inking.
Time Scrub tool
Drag across the stage to move through time.
Outliner
The whole document as a tree, down to individual paths.
Phoenix documents
A faster, more robust file format with room to grow. See The Phoenix format.
Crash recovery
Regular recovery copies, offered back after a crash.
Reference video
Import video straight onto a guide layer to animate over.
Shareable eases and presets
Export custom eases as JSON, and import Animate's motion presets.
Command-line rendering
Export and convert without opening a window. See Command line.
Free and open source
On Windows and macOS, with Linux on the way.

Not in Phoenix yet

These Animate features are not available yet. Files that use them still open, and the data is kept when you save.

  • The Camera tool and camera layers (camera layers draw as ordinary layers), and 3D rotation and translation.
  • Building bones with the Bone tool, and the Asset Warp tool. Existing armatures and warps play.
  • The Paint Brush with art and pattern brushes, and the Width tool.
  • Running ActionScript or JavaScript, and JSFL commands and extensions.
  • HTML5 Canvas, SWF and other publish targets.
