Drawing

Drawing tools

Every tool's settings live on the Properties panel's Tool tab, which appears when you pick the tool. Phoenix remembers each tool's settings between sessions.

Color and style

Tools that draw show a Color and Style section at the top of the Tool tab:

Fill and Stroke
The colors to draw with. Uncheck either one to draw without it, for example a rectangle with no outline.
Stroke size
Line thickness, from 0.1 to 200 pixels.
Style
Solid, Hairline (always one screen pixel wide), Dashed, Dotted, Ragged, Stipple or Hatched.
Cap
How open line ends look: Round, None (flat at the end point) or Square.
Join
How corners look: Round, Bevel or Miter. A miter limit stops very sharp corners from spiking.
Scale
Whether the stroke thickens when the artwork is scaled: Normal (it does), Horizontal, Vertical, or None (it stays the same width).

Pen tools

The Pen tool slot holds four tools for drawing and editing precise paths.

Pen

Press P. The Pen draws a path one point at a time.

Do thisTo
ClickPlace a corner point
DragPlace a smooth point and pull out its curve handles
Alt-dragBreak the handles so the curve can change direction sharply
ShiftKeep the segment or handle at a multiple of 45°
Click the first pointClose the path; a closed path gets a fill
Enter or double-clickFinish an open path, drawn as a stroke only
EscAbandon the path
Press on the end of an open pathCarry on drawing from there

The cursor shows what the next click will do: start a new path, close the path, or add or remove a point. If you switch tools in the middle of a path, Phoenix keeps what you have drawn as an open path.

Add, Delete and Convert Anchor Point

Add Anchor Point (=)
Click a path to add a point without changing its shape.
Delete Anchor Point (-)
Click a point to remove it. The curve is refitted so it keeps its shape as closely as it can.
Convert Anchor Point (Shift+C)
Click a smooth point to make it a corner, or drag from a corner to pull out curve handles.

To move points and handles, use the Subselection tool.

Line

Press N and drag to draw a straight line. Hold Shift to keep it at a multiple of 45°, or Alt to draw outward from the middle.

Shapes and primitives

One toolbox slot holds the shape tools. Press and hold it to choose.

ToolKeyNotes
RectangleRShift draws a square, Alt draws from the center. While dragging, press ↑ and ↓ to round the corners (with Shift for bigger steps). Corner radius sets the rounding before you draw.

Oval PrimitiveAn oval with an editable start angle, end angle and inner radius, for pie slices and rings.

Editing primitives

Select a primitive and the Object tab shows its options:

  • Rectangle Options: the radius of each corner, with Lock corners together to change all four at once.
  • Oval Options: Start and End angle, Inner radius as a percentage, and Close path.
  • Reset returns the options to a plain rectangle or full oval.

To edit a primitive's points freely, break it apart first (Ctrl+B).

Pencil

Press D and draw freehand. Phoenix shows how your line will be fitted as you draw, and fits it when you release.

Pencil mode
Smooth rounds your line into flowing curves. Straighten turns it into straight segments and clean arcs. Ink keeps it close to exactly what you drew.
Smoothing
0 to 100. Higher values remove more wobble.
Connect lines
In Straighten mode, closes a stroke whose ends nearly meet.
Recognize shapes
In Straighten mode, turns a rough rectangle or oval into a true one.

Start a stroke on the end of a selected open path to extend it.

Brushes

The brush slot (B) holds three brushes. All three paint filled shapes, use your tablet's pen pressure, and remember their settings.

Fluid Brush

A brush whose width responds to pressure and speed, with tapered ends.

Size
1 to 200 pixels.
Pressure
Lets pen pressure vary the width, down to Min size as a percentage of the full size.
Velocity
None, Thin to thick, or Thick to thin: how drawing speed changes the width.
Taper start / end
How far each end narrows to a point, 0 to 50%.
Smoothing
How much hand wobble is removed.
Precision
How closely the final shape follows the stroke, against how many points it uses.
Roundness and Angle
The shape of the tip: lower roundness gives a flat, calligraphic nib turned to the angle.

Classic Brush

A brush with a fixed tip shape: Round, Oval, Square, Rectangle, Vertical line, or one of two diagonals. It can use pen pressure for size and tilt for shape, and has its own smoothing.

Ink Brush

A brush for confident inked lines, unique to Phoenix.

Stabilizer
0 to 60 pixels. The line trails behind the cursor as if pulled on a string, which irons out shaky strokes. Higher values give smoother, slower lines.
Pressure
Soft, Linear or Firm: how quickly the line reaches full width as you press harder.
Min size
The width at the lightest pressure.
Tilt shading
Tilting the pen broadens the line, like the side of a brush.

Brush modes

Every brush has a Brush mode that limits where paint lands:

ModePaints
Paint NormalOver everything on the layer
Paint FillsOver fills and empty areas, leaving strokes on top
Paint BehindOnly on empty areas, behind existing artwork
Paint SelectionOnly inside the selected fill
Paint InsideOnly inside the fill where the stroke starts

Press Esc during a stroke to cancel it.

Planned

A Paint Brush with art and pattern brushes and a brush library, textured brushes, and a Width tool for shaping variable-width strokes.

Eraser

Press E and drag to erase merge artwork. Set its Size, and choose an Eraser mode:

ModeErases
Erase NormalFills and strokes
Erase FillsFills only
Erase LinesStrokes only
Erase Selected FillsOnly the selected fills
Erase InsideOnly inside the fill where you start

Check Faucet to delete a whole fill or stroke with one click. Turning a tablet pen over to its eraser end switches to the Eraser, and turning it back returns to your previous tool.

Paint Bucket and Ink Bottle

The Paint Bucket (K) fills an enclosed area with the fill color, or recolors an existing fill. If your outline has small gaps, choose a Gap size: Don't Close Gaps, or close small, medium or large gaps.

The Ink Bottle (S), in the same slot, works on strokes. Click a fill to add an outline around it, or click a stroke to give it the current stroke color, size and style.

Eyedropper

Press I and click a fill or stroke to take its color. After you pick up a fill, Phoenix switches to the Paint Bucket so you can apply it straight away; after a stroke, to the Ink Bottle.
