Stage, zoom and view

How to move around your artwork, line things up precisely, and control what the stage shows. None of these change your document except guides, which are saved with it.

The stage controls

The right end of the edit bar, above the stage, holds the view controls you reach for most:

Center Stage
Brings the stage to the middle of the view, keeping the zoom.
Rotation
Picks the Rotation tool. While the view is turned, the button shows the angle, and clicking it sets the view straight again.
Content outside the stage
Choose whether artwork on the pasteboard is shown, dimmed, or clipped away. This is the same as Dim Outside Document and Clip Content Outside Stage in the View menu, and Phoenix remembers your choice. Exports are never affected.
Zoom box
Shows the current zoom and follows every other way of zooming. Type a percentage, use its arrows (or the Up and Down keys) to step through the set sizes, or open the menu beside it for Fit in Window, Show Frame, Show All and the set sizes.

The Zoom tool

Press Z for the Zoom tool. Click to zoom in two times at that point, Alt-click to zoom out, or drag a rectangle to zoom to exactly that area.

Pan

  • The mouse wheel scrolls up and down; Shift with the wheel scrolls sideways.
  • Hold Space and drag with any tool, or drag with the middle mouse button.
  • Use the Hand tool (H) and drag.

Rotate the view

Turning the canvas makes it easier to draw natural strokes at any angle. The Rotation tool (Shift+H) shares a slot with the Hand tool.

  • Click to set the point to turn around, then drag to turn the view.
  • Hold Shift to turn in 15° steps.
  • Double-click with the tool, click the angle shown on the edit bar, or choose View › Reset Rotation, to straighten the view again.

Rotating the view never rotates your artwork. Rulers are hidden while the view is turned.

Scrub through time on the stage

The Time Scrub tool, also in the Hand tool's slot, moves the playhead as you drag left and right across the stage, one frame for every few pixels. It is a quick way to check motion without reaching for the timeline.

The camera

The Camera tool (C), the last in the Hand tool's slot, moves the scene's camera rather than your view of it: drag to pan, Shift-drag to zoom in and out, and Alt-drag to turn it about the middle of the stage. Each move is keyed on the frame shown, so the camera animates from one key to the next. If the scene has no camera yet, the first drag adds one (as Insert › Camera does).

With View › Camera View on, as it is by default, the stage shows the scene through its camera. Turn it off to see the scene as laid out, without the camera's moves. For layers at different depths from the camera, see Multiplane camera.

Rulers and guides

Turn on rulers with View › Rulers (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R). Rulers measure from the stage's top-left corner, in the units set in Modify › Document (pixels unless you change them).

  1. Drag from the top ruler to create a horizontal guide, or from the left ruler for a vertical one.
  2. Drag a guide with the Selection tool to move it.
  3. Drag it back onto a ruler, or out of the view, to remove it.

Guides are saved with the document, and creating, moving or removing one can be undone. The View › Guides menu has:

Show Guides
Ctrl+;. Hides guides without deleting them.
Snap to Guides
Ctrl+Shift+;. Artwork snaps to guides as you drag it.
Lock Guides
Ctrl+Alt+;. Guides stay where they are while you work near them.
Edit Guides...
Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G. The guides' color, whether they show, snap and are locked, and Clear All. The color is saved with the document.
Clear Guides
Removes every guide. This can be undone too.

Grid and snapping

Snapping helps you place artwork exactly. While snapping is happening, a small ring appears under the cursor at the point it snapped to. Each kind can be turned on and off in the View menu:

OptionDefaultSnaps to
Snap to ObjectsOnThe corners, edges and centers of other artwork
Snap to GuidesOnRuler guides
Snap to GridOffThe document grid (Ctrl+Shift+'). Use Show Grid (Ctrl+') to see it.
Snap to PixelsOffWhole pixels, for crisp artwork at 100%

View › Edit Grid (Ctrl+Alt+G) sets the grid's color and its spacing across and down, saved with the document, and whether it shows, shows over the artwork instead of under it, and snaps. Ruler units are in Modify › Document.

Display options

Clip Content Outside Stage
Hides whatever lies off the stage, so you see what will be exported.
Pasteboard
On by default. Turn it off to show only the stage, with nothing around it.
Center Stage
Brings the stage to the middle of the view, keeping the zoom.
Dim Outside Document
Darkens the pasteboard so you can judge what is on the stage. Artwork off the stage still draws, so you can grab it. Phoenix remembers this setting.
High Quality Anti-Aliasing
On by default. Turn it off to draw faster in very heavy scenes.
Show Bounding Boxes
Ctrl+Shift+B. Outlines each element's bounds, which helps track down a stray point far from the artwork.
Onion Skin
See Onion skinning.
Light Table
Shift+L. Fades every layer except the one you are drawing on. See Light table.
View Document...
Opens a read-only window with the document's underlying XML, for the curious and for troubleshooting.

Scenes

A document can be split into scenes, which play one after another. Phoenix shows one scene at a time. Switch scenes from View › Scene or the Window › Scene panel (Shift+F2), or press Shift+Page Up and Shift+Page Down for the previous and next scene. The current scene's name appears in the edit bar above the stage. To add, rename, reorder and delete scenes, see Scenes; to export every scene at once, see Export Movie.
