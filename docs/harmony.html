Reference

Coming from Harmony

Phoenix brings several of Harmony's ways of working to FLA files. What Animate cannot show is kept in the file where Animate ignores it, so the files still open there.

X-sheet

Window > X-Sheet shows the timeline as an exposure sheet: a column per layer, a row per frame, each drawing numbered by its content. Type a drawing number into a cell to expose that drawing there, x to hold, or Delete to clear.

Pegs

A peg is a layer that holds only a transform. Pick a layer and choose Insert > Peg: a Peg layer appears above it and the layer is parented to it. Move, turn or scale the peg (with the Free Transform tool, or tween it) and everything parented to it follows, about the peg's pivot. Parent more layers to the same peg from the layer menu's Parent Layer, or parent pegs to pegs to build a hierarchy.

On the stage a peg shows as a crosshair in a circle, in its layer's color, with a tick for the way it faces. In the file it is an instance of an empty graphic symbol on a layer Animate's own layer parenting follows, so Animate plays the animation the same and draws nothing for the peg.

Line and color art

Keep a drawing's clean lines on one layer and its paint on the layer under it, so painting never disturbs a line and lines can be redrawn without losing the paint. Pick the layer with the drawing and choose Insert > Color Art Layer: its fills move to a new layer under it, named after it with Color, and the strokes stay where they are.

On the color art layer, the Paint Bucket fills the regions the line layer's strokes close around where you click, even where the color layer has nothing yet, so you can ink first and paint after. The paint lands on the color layer and the lines stay untouched above it. Both are ordinary layers in the saved file.

Tone, highlight and glow

Pick the layer to shade and choose Insert > Effect Layer: Tone, Highlight or Glow. An effect layer appears above it; draw the shape of the shadow or light on it with any tool. Phoenix softens the shape, colors it and composites it over the layer under it: a tone is multiplied, darkening what is beneath; a highlight is screened, lightening it; a glow is added. A tone or highlight is kept within the layer's art, so a shadow drawn roughly over a character never spills off it; a glow spreads outside.

Right-click the effect layer and choose Effect Settings to change its color, softness, strength and whether it is kept within the layer. While you draw on it, its shape shows faintly so you can see what you draw. Effects render on the stage and in everything Phoenix exports. In the saved file an effect layer is a guide layer, which Animate leaves out rather than drawing its shape flat.

Linked palettes

Open Window > Palettes. A palette is a set of named colors (skin, hair, outline, shadow). Add makes a color from the current fill color. Select art and choose Fill or Stroke to color it with the palette color and link it; Link Matching links every fill, stroke and text in the document that already has that color, which is the quick way to bring existing art into a palette. The number beside each color is how many uses are linked to it.

Edit (or double-click a color) changes it everywhere it is linked, as one undo step. From the ... menu, New Palette makes a copy of the palette in use under a new name, such as Night: recolor its colors, and switching palettes in the list recolors the whole document. The colors are written into the art itself, so Animate shows it as last colored; the palettes and the links are kept where Animate ignores them.

Master controllers

A controller is one slider that drives several properties at once: a head turn that moves the head, swaps the eyes' drawing and turns the nose. Open Window > Controllers and choose New. Then, for each thing it should drive, pick the layer in the timeline, select the controller and choose Bind: choose the property (X, Y, rotation, scale, alpha, or a graphic's frame) and the values it should have with the slider at 0 and at 1. Both start at the value the layer has now.

Dragging the slider sets every property it drives on the frame shown, as one undo step. A layer with no keyframe there gets one; cut into a tween, the new keyframe takes the in-between and both halves keep tweening. What is saved is ordinary keyframes, so Animate plays it as you see it; the controllers themselves are kept in the document where Animate ignores them.

Function curves

Window > Function Curves shows one property of the layer picked in the timeline as a curve over its frames: X, Y, rotation, scale, skew, alpha, brightness, tint or a graphic's frame, or the camera's pan, zoom and turn on a camera layer. The curve runs through the tweens between keyframes, as they play, and each keyframe is a dot.

Drag a dot up or down to change that keyframe's value, or double-click it to type one; both are one undo step. Click anywhere else in the graph to go to that frame.

Light table

View > Light Table (Shift+L) fades every layer except the one you are drawing on, so you can see the other layers through it without mistaking them for your own.

Multiplane camera

Insert > Camera adds a camera to the scene; Window > Layer Depth sets how far each layer sits from it, so a pan moves near layers faster than far ones. The Camera tool (C) pans, zooms and turns it, keyed on the frame shown.
